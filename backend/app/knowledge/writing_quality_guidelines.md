# Writing Quality Guidelines

## What a strong essay looks like

- It opens with an introduction that engages with the prompt and states a clear thesis.
- Each body paragraph begins with a topic sentence, develops one idea and supports it with a specific example, fact or explanation.
- It acknowledges complexity, for example by addressing a counter-argument, where the prompt invites it.
- It ends with a conclusion that draws the argument together rather than merely repeating the introduction.

## Paragraphing and organization

- A typical short academic essay has four to six paragraphs: an introduction, two to four body paragraphs and a conclusion.
- Body paragraphs normally run from about 60 to 150 words. Much shorter paragraphs are usually underdeveloped; much longer ones usually contain more than one idea.
- Ideas should be ordered deliberately: by importance, chronologically, or from problem to solution.

## Sentence quality

- Good writing mixes short and long sentences. An average sentence length of roughly 15 to 25 words is typical of clear academic prose.
- Common sentence faults are run-on sentences, comma splices, sentence fragments and inconsistent verb tense.
- Punctuation should clarify meaning. Overuse of exclamation marks or missing commas after introductory phrases weakens formal writing.

## Word choice and tone

- Prefer precise words to vague ones such as "things", "stuff", "good", "bad", "a lot".
- Avoid repeating the same word or phrase many times; use synonyms or restructure the sentence.
- Academic tone avoids slang, text-message abbreviations and excessive contractions, and avoids addressing the reader as "you" without purpose.
- Ambitious vocabulary only earns credit when it is used accurately.

## Use of evidence

- Claims should be backed by specific examples, data, well-known facts or logical reasoning.
- Evidence must be relevant to the claim and explained, not simply dropped into the paragraph.
- Personal experience is acceptable support when the prompt allows it, but should be linked back to the general argument.
